# Photo to Editorial Poster

Turn each uploaded photograph into a **separate, high-end design poster**. Do NOT combine multiple photos into a collage; output **one poster per photo**.

Each poster uses an overall **3:4 vertical composition**, strictly divided into **two equal sections** — the top and bottom each occupy **exactly 50%** of the canvas height.

---

## Composition Blueprint

- Canvas ratio: **3:4 vertical** (portrait)
- The canvas is split into two halves of exactly equal height: a **photographic top half** and an **illustrated bottom half**
- Never crop the illustration into the photo or overlap the two halves
- Keep the whole composition balanced, airy, and spacious, with a strong sense of breathing room

---

## Top Half — The Photograph

The top half preserves the **original photograph**.

Requirements:

- Maintain the subject's structure, realistic texture, natural lighting, and original color atmosphere
- Apply only **subtle, refined photographic color grading** — the quality of editorial magazine photography and fine-art exhibition imagery
- When the original aspect ratio does not match the required frame, **naturally extend the sky, ground, or surrounding environment** to adapt the image — never stretch, distort, or alter the main subject
- Do not cartoonize, illustrate, or over-process the photo; it should still read as a beautiful photograph

---

## Bottom Half — The Illustration

Extract the **most recognizable subject, silhouette, pose, and narrative relationships** from the photograph, then reinterpret them as a **light, naive, retro hand-drawn editorial illustration**.

### Reinterpretation, Not Tracing

- Do NOT mechanically trace or reproduce every detail
- Reinterpret the subject through:
  - simplified forms
  - slightly exaggerated proportions
  - symbolic features
  - humorous visual metaphors
- The original subject must remain **instantly recognizable**

### Visual Language

Blend the aesthetics of:

- modernist editorial illustration
- Bauhaus composition
- children's picture books
- fashion sketching

Keep the forms concise and the lines **thin, delicate, and slightly imperfect by hand**. The overall feeling should be relaxed, intelligent, playful, and restrained.

---

## Color Palette

Derive the color palette from the photograph above.

- Use a **limited palette** with **high lightness and low-to-medium saturation**
- Leave **generous areas of off-white or pale negative space**
- Use **small touches of complementary color** as visual accents

---

## Texture & Finish

Use a mixed-media texture inspired by:

- watercolor
- gouache
- colored pencil
- oil pastel
- dry-brush techniques

Preserve visible:

- paper grain
- rough edges
- areas of exposed paper
- slight misregistration (out-of-register printing)
- imperfect brushwork

**Avoid**: smooth vector graphics, photorealistic lighting, 3D rendering, and plastic-looking surfaces.

---

## Supporting Details

- A small number of **hand-drawn lines, geometric symbols, or abstract elements** may be added
- Use typography **sparingly** — for example retro handwritten titles, numbers, or years — naturally integrated into the illustration
- Typography should feel hand-set, nostalgic, and editorial, not like a marketing template

---

## Visual Direction

The overall visual direction should reference:

- vintage fashion picture books
- travel illustration
- art publications
- sophisticated editorial design

Create a mood that feels: gentle, nostalgic, relaxed, stylish, humorous, slightly awkward, and confidently eccentric.

**Avoid** anything that feels overly cartoonish, cheap, commercial/e-commerce-oriented, or template-driven.

---

## Multiple Photos

When the user uploads multiple photos:

- Generate **one poster per photo**
- Do not merge photos into a collage
- Apply the same poster system, proportions, and visual language across the series so the posters feel like a coherent set while each remains independent
